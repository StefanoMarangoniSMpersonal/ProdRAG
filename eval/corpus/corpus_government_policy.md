## Country Profile

The Republic of Valdoria is a parliamentary republic located in Central Europe with a population of 6.8 million (2024 census). The capital city is Kirova, which has a population of 1.2 million. The official language is Valdorian, though 74% of the population also speaks English. The current Prime Minister is Elina Voss, elected in 2023. The currency is the Valdorian Krone (VKR); 1 USD ≈ 5.3 VKR as of 2024.

## Regulation 1: The Digital Privacy and Data Sovereignty Act (DPDSA)

### Overview

The DPDSA was enacted on September 1, 2022, making Valdoria one of the first countries to require that all personal data of Valdorian citizens be stored on servers physically located within Valdorian borders. The law was drafted by the Ministry of Technology under then-Minister Aleksander Bruk.

### Key Provisions

Companies processing data of more than 10,000 Valdorian citizens must register with the National Data Authority (NDA) and submit to annual compliance audits. Non-compliance penalties start at 50,000 VKR for a first offense, escalating to 500,000 VKR or 4% of annual Valdorian revenue (whichever is greater) for repeated violations.

The law includes a 24-month grace period for foreign companies, which expired on September 1, 2024. After the grace period, three major international firms were fined: CloudNet Solutions (fined 320,000 VKR for storing citizen health records in Frankfurt), DataStream AG (fined 180,000 VKR for delayed registration), and social media platform Chirpr (fined 1.2 million VKR for failure to appoint a local data representative).

### Exemptions

Government agencies, hospitals, and financial institutions regulated by the Valdorian Central Bank are exempt from the registration fee (normally 8,000 VKR annually) but are still subject to audits. Academic research institutions can apply for a "Research Data Exemption" that allows temporary cross-border data transfer for up to 6 months with NDA pre-approval.

## Regulation 2: The Renewable Energy Mandate (REM)

### Background

In 2021, Valdoria passed the Renewable Energy Mandate, requiring that 70% of national electricity generation come from renewable sources by 2030. At the time of passage, renewables accounted for 43% of the energy mix: 28% hydroelectric, 11% wind, and 4% solar.

### Targets and Progress

| Year | Target (%) | Actual (%) | Primary Growth Source |
|------|-----------|------------|----------------------|
| 2022 | 48        | 49         | Wind (+3%)           |
| 2023 | 53        | 55         | Wind (+4%), Solar (+2%) |
| 2024 | 58        | 57         | Solar (+3%)          |

The country slightly missed its 2024 target due to a drought that reduced hydroelectric output by 6% compared to historical averages. The government responded by fast-tracking permits for two offshore wind farms in the Kessler Bay, expected to add 340 MW of capacity by 2026.

### Subsidies and Incentives

Households installing rooftop solar panels receive a one-time rebate of 3,500 VKR (approximately $660 USD) and a guaranteed feed-in tariff of 0.18 VKR per kWh for surplus energy sold back to the grid. As of 2024, approximately 42,000 households have taken advantage of the program, representing 8.2% of all residential properties.

Commercial buildings over 500 m² of roof area are required to install solar panels or green roofs. Non-compliance results in an annual surcharge of 12 VKR per m² of non-compliant roof area.

## Regulation 3: The Small Business Protection Act (SBPA)

### Overview

The SBPA, passed in March 2023 under PM Elina Voss's administration, provides targeted support for businesses with fewer than 25 employees and annual revenue below 2 million VKR.

### Key Provisions

Qualifying businesses receive a reduced corporate tax rate of 12% (compared to the standard rate of 21%). They are also exempt from the employer portion of social insurance contributions for the first two employees hired each calendar year, for a period of 18 months per hire.

The act established the Valdorian Small Business Fund (VSBF), initially capitalized at 150 million VKR, which provides low-interest loans (2.1% APR) with repayment terms of up to 7 years. In its first year, the VSBF disbursed 1,847 loans totaling 89 million VKR. The average loan size was 48,200 VKR, and the default rate after one year was 3.1%.

### Criticism

The Valdorian Chamber of Commerce criticized the SBPA for creating a "cliff effect" — businesses approaching the 25-employee threshold avoid hiring to remain eligible. A 2024 analysis by the Kirova School of Economics found that 14% of qualifying businesses maintained exactly 24 employees, compared to an expected 6% based on normal size distributions.

## Regulation 4: Immigration and Skilled Worker Visa Program

### The Blue Card Program

In 2020, Valdoria introduced the "Blue Card" visa for skilled foreign workers in technology, healthcare, and engineering. Applicants must have a job offer with a minimum salary of 180,000 VKR per year (approximately $34,000 USD) and a recognized university degree or equivalent professional certification.

Blue Card holders receive a 3-year renewable work permit. After 5 continuous years of residence and passing a Valdorian language proficiency exam (Level B2), they are eligible to apply for permanent residency. Spouses of Blue Card holders receive automatic work authorization.

The shift toward healthcare in 2023 was driven by a national nursing shortage. The Ministry of Health launched a targeted recruitment campaign in the Philippines and recruited 860 nurses over a 9-month period.

## Regulation 5: The Cultural Heritage Preservation Decree

### Background

Issued by executive decree in November 2022, this regulation protects structures older than 100 years within the historic centers of Kirova, Petrov, and Dragenholm. Building owners cannot alter the exterior facades of protected structures without approval from the Heritage Council, a 7-member panel appointed by the Ministry of Culture.

Applications for exterior modification are reviewed within 90 days. The approval rate in 2023 was 34% — the majority of rejections involved proposed replacement of original wooden window frames with modern PVC alternatives. The Heritage Council has granted 12 full demolition permits since the decree's inception, all for structures deemed structurally unsafe by independent engineers.

Property owners of protected buildings can apply for a Heritage Maintenance Grant of up to 25,000 VKR per year toward restoration costs. In 2024, the government allocated 18 million VKR to the grant program, serving 940 properties.
