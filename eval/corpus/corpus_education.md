## University Overview

Thornfield University is a private research university located in Millhaven, a fictional college town on the eastern seaboard of the United States. It was founded in 1891 by industrialist Arthur Thornfield, who donated $2 million (equivalent to approximately $68 million today) and 240 acres of land for the campus.

As of 2024, Thornfield has an enrollment of 14,600 students: 9,200 undergraduates and 5,400 graduate/professional students. The university employs 1,100 faculty members and has a student-to-faculty ratio of 11:1. Its endowment stands at $4.3 billion. The current president is Dr. Naomi Chen, who took office in 2022 after serving as provost at the University of Wisconsin.

## Academic Programs

### Schools and Colleges

Thornfield University comprises six schools:

1. **College of Arts and Sciences** — the largest, with 4,800 undergraduates
2. **School of Engineering** — 2,200 students (undergraduate and graduate combined)
3. **Thornfield Business School** — 1,800 students
4. **School of Public Health** — 1,400 graduate students only
5. **School of Law** — 620 students
6. **School of Education** — 480 students

### Flagship Programs

The university is particularly known for its biomedical engineering program, which was ranked #14 nationally in the 2024 Harmon University Rankings. The program is led by Dr. Marcus Hale, who holds the Thornfield Chair in Biomedical Innovation. In 2023, the program received $18.2 million in NIH-funded research grants.

The School of Public Health's epidemiology concentration is another standout, ranked #22 nationally. It was instrumental during the 2020 pandemic response, contributing 4 peer-reviewed papers on vaccine distribution modeling to the *Journal of Public Health Policy*.

The law school has a first-time bar passage rate of 89% (2023), above the national average of 79%.

Thornfield practices need-blind admissions for domestic applicants and meets 100% of demonstrated financial need. The average financial aid package for aided students is $52,400 per year. Approximately 58% of undergraduates receive some form of institutional aid.

### Graduate Admissions

Each school manages its own graduate admissions. The most selective is the School of Public Health, which accepted 12% of applicants in 2024. The Business School (MBA program) accepted 24% of applicants, with a median GMAT score of 718.

## Campus and Facilities

The main campus spans 310 acres and includes 78 buildings. Notable facilities include:

- **Thornfield Library** — houses 2.1 million volumes and 480 electronic databases. A $45 million renovation completed in 2020 added a 24-hour study wing and a digital media lab.
- **Hale Biomedical Research Center** — a 120,000 sq ft facility opened in 2018, home to 14 research labs and a Level 3 biosafety laboratory.
- **Weaver Athletic Complex** — includes a 6,500-seat basketball arena, an Olympic-standard swimming pool, and a 400-meter indoor track. Built in 2005, renovated in 2019.
- **The Quad** — the historic center of campus, a 4-acre green space surrounded by the original 1891 sandstone buildings. It hosts the annual Founders' Day ceremony each October.

| Source                     | Amount (USD) | Share |
|----------------------------|-------------|-------|
| Federal grants (NIH, NSF, DOE) | 198 million | 63%   |
| Industry partnerships      | 62 million  | 20%   |
| Foundations and nonprofits  | 34 million  | 11%   |
| State government           | 18 million  | 6%    |

The university holds 214 active patents and generated $8.6 million in licensing revenue in 2024. The most commercially successful patent is for a biodegradable surgical mesh developed by the biomedical engineering department in 2019, which has been licensed to three medical device companies.

## Athletics

Thornfield competes in NCAA Division I as a member of the Coastal Athletic Conference (CAC). The school's teams are called the Thornfield Ospreys. The school colors are navy blue and copper.

The most successful program is women's lacrosse, which has won 5 CAC championships (2015, 2017, 2019, 2022, 2024) and reached the NCAA tournament quarterfinals twice. The head coach is Dana Morretti, who has been with the program since 2013.

Men's basketball had its most successful season in 2023, winning 24 games and earning the program's first NCAA tournament bid. They lost in the first round to a #3 seed.

The university does not have a football program.

## Notable Alumni

- **Dr. Lydia Wren** (Class of 1952): Pioneering cardiologist who developed the Wren Valve, an early prosthetic heart valve used in over 50,000 surgeries before newer designs replaced it in the 1980s.
- **Congressman Robert Huxley** (Class of 1978): Represented the 9th Congressional District for 7 terms (1994–2008), known for authoring the Clean Water Restoration Act of 2002.
- **Samira Osei** (Class of 2005): CEO of Helix Therapeutics, a biotech company valued at $2.1 billion in 2024. Named to Forbes "40 Under 40" in 2019.
- **Evan Gallagher** (Class of 2011): Olympic silver medalist in the 200m butterfly (2016 Summer Olympics). Currently an assistant swim coach at Thornfield.

The total cost of attendance has increased at an average rate of 3.4% per year over the past decade. In response to affordability concerns, President Chen announced the "Thornfield Promise" in 2023: families earning below $75,000 per year pay no tuition, and families earning below $150,000 pay no more than $15,000 per year.
