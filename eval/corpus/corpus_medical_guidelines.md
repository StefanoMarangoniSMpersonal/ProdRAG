## Institution Profile

Lakeview Medical Center (LMC) is a 620-bed teaching hospital located in Briarfield, a fictional mid-size city in the Pacific Northwest. Founded in 1971, it is affiliated with the Briarfield College of Medicine. LMC employs 3,400 staff, including 480 physicians and 1,100 nurses. The current Chief Medical Officer is Dr. Helen Quarles, who assumed the role in 2021.

LMC is the regional trauma center (Level II) and operates the only pediatric intensive care unit (PICU) within a 150 km radius. In 2024, the hospital recorded 42,000 inpatient admissions, 128,000 emergency department visits, and 18,000 surgical procedures.

## Protocol 1: Sepsis Early Detection Bundle (SEDB)

### Background

In 2019, LMC implemented the Sepsis Early Detection Bundle after an internal review found that sepsis mortality at the hospital was 19.4%, compared to a national benchmark of 15.2%. The protocol was developed by Dr. Rajiv Menon, head of the Critical Care Division.

### Trigger Criteria

The SEDB is activated when a patient presents with at least two of the following:

- Temperature above 38.3°C or below 36.0°C
- Heart rate above 90 beats per minute
- Respiratory rate above 20 breaths per minute
- White blood cell count above 12,000/μL or below 4,000/μL
- Systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg

### Required Actions (within 60 minutes of activation)

1. Draw blood cultures (at least 2 sets from separate sites) before antibiotic administration.
2. Measure serum lactate level.
3. Administer broad-spectrum IV antibiotics.
4. Begin IV fluid resuscitation with 30 mL/kg crystalloid if lactate is ≥ 4 mmol/L or systolic BP remains below 90 mmHg after initial assessment.
5. Page the on-call intensivist for assessment.

### Outcomes

After implementation, LMC's sepsis mortality dropped from 19.4% to 13.8% within 18 months. Compliance with the 60-minute antibiotic target improved from 64% to 91%. The protocol was presented at the National Critical Care Conference in 2021 and has since been adopted by 14 other hospitals in the region.

## Protocol 2: Pediatric Asthma Pathway

### Overview

LMC's Pediatric Asthma Pathway was introduced in 2020 to standardize care for children aged 2–17 presenting with acute asthma exacerbations in the emergency department. It was designed by Dr. Sonia Park, chief of pediatric pulmonology.

### Severity Classification

| Severity | Oxygen Saturation | Peak Flow (% predicted) | Speech Pattern |
|----------|-------------------|------------------------|----------------|
| Mild     | ≥ 94%             | ≥ 70%                  | Full sentences |
| Moderate | 90–93%            | 50–69%                 | Phrases only   |
| Severe   | < 90%             | < 50%                  | Words only     |

### Treatment by Severity

**Mild:** Albuterol nebulization (2.5 mg) every 20 minutes for up to 3 doses. If oxygen saturation remains ≥ 94% after treatment, discharge with a 5-day oral prednisolone course (1 mg/kg/day, max 40 mg).

**Moderate:** Albuterol nebulization plus ipratropium bromide (0.5 mg) for the first 3 doses. Administer oral or IV corticosteroids. Reassess after 1 hour; if not improving, escalate to severe pathway.

**Severe:** Continuous albuterol nebulization. IV methylprednisolone (2 mg/kg, max 60 mg). Consider IV magnesium sulfate (40 mg/kg, max 2 g) if not responsive to initial treatment. Immediate PICU consultation.

### Results

Since implementation, PICU admissions for asthma have decreased by 23%. The average emergency department length of stay for pediatric asthma cases dropped from 4.2 hours to 2.9 hours. Readmission within 72 hours remained stable at 4.1%.

## Protocol 3: Surgical Site Infection Prevention (SSIP)

### Scope

The SSIP protocol applies to all elective surgeries performed at LMC. It was introduced in 2018 by the Infection Control Committee, chaired by Dr. Miriam Okafor.

### Key Measures

- **Pre-operative skin preparation:** Chlorhexidine-alcohol antiseptic applied 30 minutes before incision. Iodine-based alternatives are used for patients with documented chlorhexidine allergy (approximately 2.3% of LMC's surgical population).
- **Antibiotic prophylaxis:** IV cefazolin (2 g for patients ≥ 80 kg, 3 g for patients ≥ 120 kg) administered within 60 minutes of incision. Re-dosed every 4 hours during prolonged procedures.
- **Normothermia maintenance:** Patient core temperature maintained between 36.0°C and 37.5°C throughout surgery using forced-air warming blankets.
- **Glucose control:** Blood glucose maintained below 10 mmol/L for diabetic patients during and 24 hours after surgery.

### Compliance and Outcomes

A 2023 audit of 5,200 elective surgeries found an overall compliance rate of 94% with all four SSIP measures. The surgical site infection rate at LMC was 1.7%, compared to a national average of 2.8%. The most common infection site was superficial incisional (58% of all SSIs), followed by deep incisional (27%) and organ/space (15%).

## Protocol 4: Falls Prevention Program

### Background

Falls are the leading cause of injury among hospitalized patients at LMC. In 2022, there were 186 reported patient falls, of which 31 (16.7%) resulted in moderate-to-severe injury. The Falls Prevention Program was launched in January 2023.

### Risk Assessment

All admitted patients are assessed using the Briarfield Falls Scale (BFS), a proprietary tool developed by LMC's nursing research team. The BFS evaluates six factors: age, medication profile (sedatives, opioids, antihypertensives), mobility status, cognitive impairment, fall history, and continence status. Each factor is scored 0–3, yielding a total score of 0–18.

| BFS Score | Risk Level | Interventions |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| 0–5       | Low       | Standard precautions |
| 6–10      | Moderate  | Non-slip footwear, bed alarm, supervised ambulation |
| 11–18     | High      | All moderate interventions plus 1:1 sitter, room near nursing station, hourly rounding |

### Outcomes

In the first full year (2023), total patient falls decreased from 186 to 134 (a 28% reduction). Falls with moderate-to-severe injury dropped from 31 to 18 (a 42% reduction). The program received the Regional Patient Safety Innovation Award in 2024.
